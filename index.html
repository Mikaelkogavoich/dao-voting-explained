How DAO voting works now

DAO votes now happen on the governance platform, which the team approved for continued use on 22 September 2026. Snapshot is no longer used. This guide covers the rules as the platform applies them today.

Can I vote?

Yes, if your wallet was enabled at Redbelly Access before the proposal's snapshot was taken.

When is it checked?

Once per proposal, at its snapshot block. That block is recorded when the proposal is published.

How many votes?

One per verified wallet on Verified identity proposals. The vote panel shows your number before you sign.

What does it cost?

Nothing. A vote is a signed message, not a transaction. You pay no gas.

01

Before your first vote

Only wallets that hold the Redbelly Network Access Credential can vote. The voting platform calls it a Receptor credential, after Redbelly's identity system. You get it once, at Redbelly Access:

  1. Connect your wallet. The portal names MetaMask and Coinbase Wallet. The address you connect becomes your public address on Redbelly Network.
  2. Verify your identity. Averer, an accredited issuer, does the check. It is free: the issuer covers the fees.
  3. Enable the account. You sign a transaction in your wallet. From then on, it can vote on every proposal whose snapshot block comes after that moment. Section 03 explains the snapshot.
02

How to vote, step by step

  1. Connect. Open redbelly-governance.vercel.app and press Connect wallet. Choose Browser wallet, or WalletConnect for a phone wallet. If you see Switch to Redbelly Network, press it.
  2. Open a proposal. Under Proposals, open one that is active. The Cast your vote panel only appears while voting is open.
  3. Check the panel. It should say Eligible to vote and show your voting power. Any other message is explained in section 06.
  4. Choose. Pick your option. You can add a reason. It is optional, and it is saved with your ballot where anyone can read it.
  5. Review and sign. Press Review and sign. The Confirm your vote box shows your choice, voting power and reason. Press Sign in wallet and approve the request in your wallet.
  6. Done. The panel shows Vote recorded. A View IPFS receipt link appears once the receipt is published. Until then the panel says the receipt is not available yet, and your vote is still recorded.
Changed your mind?

Until voting closes, press Change vote and sign again. The new ballot replaces the old one.

What you sign

A message with your address, the proposal, your choice, your reason and a timestamp. There is no amount and no recipient, so nothing can leave your wallet. If anything asks you to approve a token or pay gas to vote, it is not the voting platform. Its Wallet safety page shows the exact message.

03

When eligibility is checked

Every proposal has a snapshot block: a block on Redbelly Network that the platform records when the proposal is published. Voting opens at publication and runs for the number of days its author set.

The platform checks your wallet once per proposal, as it was at that block. In its own words:

"Eligibility is based on the proposal snapshot. Changes after that snapshot do not add voting power."

The snapshot block comes slightly before voting opens. On the 10 proposals published on the platform so far, the gap was 15 to 162 seconds.

04

Why a wallet verified after a proposal opens cannot vote on it

A wallet enabled after the snapshot block did not hold the credential at that block. For that proposal, the platform reads it as unverified, and its voting power is 0.

Nothing done during the vote changes this, because the check is tied to a block that is already in the past. The rule is the same for every wallet, so the list of eligible wallets stays fixed from the first ballot to the last.

Read this before you worry

The message you see is This address does not hold a Receptor credential on Redbelly, even if your wallet is verified today. On this proposal it means "not verified at the snapshot". We tested this with a wallet enabled 3 blocks after a snapshot: the platform lists it as verified, and shows that message for the proposal.

The wallet is not blocked. It can vote on every proposal whose snapshot comes after it was enabled.

05

How many votes you get

One vote per verified account

Every proposal published on the platform today is open to verified wallets only. The DAO's votes since the platform was approved use the Verified identity setting: each wallet that was verified at the snapshot gets exactly 1 vote, however much RBNT it holds.

The platform also supports measuring power by RBNT balance. On such a proposal, your power is the RBNT your wallet held at the snapshot block, and only verified wallets can vote. Either way, the vote panel shows your voting power before you sign.

Some proposals let you approve several options, split your vote across options, or rank them. The panel shows how the proposal you open works.

Several wallets under one verification

After your first wallet is enabled, Redbelly Access lets you add more accounts linked to the same identity. The platform treats each enabled wallet as a separate verified address:

  • Each wallet that was enabled at the snapshot can cast 1 vote on that proposal.
  • A wallet linked after the snapshot cannot vote on that proposal.
  • The Voters and Identity quorum numbers count addresses. The platform states that verified addresses "are not necessarily unique people", and that "one credential currently enables up to about 10 addresses".

07

Known limits

These are how the platform works today. Knowing them saves a lot of confusion.

  • Addresses, not people. One credential currently enables up to about 10 addresses, according to the platform. Vote counts and the identity quorum count verified addresses, not unique people.
  • No late entry. A wallet enabled after a proposal's snapshot cannot vote on it, even by a few minutes. The snapshot can be up to 162 seconds before voting opens.
  • The not-eligible message does not mention the snapshot. A wallet verified after the snapshot is told it does not hold a Receptor credential, although it holds one now.
  • Ballots are signed messages, not transactions. The platform stores them. Each ballot carries your signature, so anyone can check it, and an IPFS receipt is published for it. The receipt can appear after the vote is recorded.
  • Two wallets named for access. Redbelly Access names MetaMask and Coinbase Wallet. The voting platform itself connects through a browser wallet or WalletConnect.
  • Settings vary by proposal. Each proposal's author sets its ballot type, voting power, quorum and length. A proposal cannot be edited once it is open.
  • Older results used older rules. Proposals marked imported came from Snapshot and were counted under that setup. Their numbers are not comparable with one vote per verified wallet.
08

Words used here

Snapshot block
The block a proposal records when it is published. Eligibility and voting power are read at this block.
Receptor credential
The platform's name for the Redbelly Network Access Credential, issued after identity verification. Receptor is Redbelly's identity and credential system.
Verified wallet
A wallet enabled at Redbelly Access, so it holds that credential. "Enabled" and "verified" mean the same here.
Signature
Your wallet's proof that a message came from you. Signing is free and moves nothing.
IPFS receipt
A public copy of your signed ballot that anyone can inspect.
Quorum
The minimum voting power a proposal needs. Identity quorum is the minimum number of verified addresses that must vote.
